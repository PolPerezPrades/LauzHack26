## 5. Decisions

- **D-A201 · 2026-11-22 · How holding works.** A pointer down on the current card (where a drag could start) freezes the countdown and its bar. Letting go without dragging resumes it with the time left. A drag keeps it frozen; a spring-back, or a drag the browser cancels, restarts it from the beginning; any exit cancels it. A result that arrives while the card is held waits for the release. — *Why:* SPEC §3.4 says "pauses" for the hold and "starts again from the beginning" only for the spring-back. Resuming lets a learner who held the card to read move on as soon as they let go. — active
- **D-A202 · 2026-11-22 · A hidden page freezes the countdown too;** back on the page, the wait restarts from the beginning. — *Why:* a correction nobody saw must not leave on its own (a tab or app switch, a locked phone). Not in the spec; it costs nothing when the page stays visible. — active
- **D-A203 · 2026-11-22 · "What was wrong" is a "You chose: …" line in the wrong-answer banner**, taken from the result itself: its only wrong retrieval, when that is a `choice` check with `confusedWith` (`feed/feedback.ts`). No contract change. The line doesn't show for other check modes (camera, self), for games, or for cards with several mistakes; those explain their mistakes in the note. The label keeps the language the banner first appeared in, like the answer and the note (D-040). — *Why:* SPEC §3.4 lists "not quite", what was wrong, the answer and the note as separate parts of the banner. The evidence already records what was picked, while a new `TaskFeedback` field would have needed Part B's views. — active
- **D-A204 · 2026-11-22 · The wrong-answer hint is now "Hold the card to keep reading"** (it replaces "Swipe or tap Next when you're ready"). It shows on screens taller than 600 px; Part A hid its hint up to 700 px, which is most phones in a browser. While the banner shows, the viewer area clips its own controls and credit as a last resort, instead of drawing over the options. — *Why:* holding is now the only way to keep a correction on screen, and nothing else teaches it. The clipping fixes an overlap seen at 360×640 with a phrase's long note and options that wrap to three lines. — active
- **D-A205 · 2026-11-22 · Enter and Space skip the wait even when focus is on a disabled control;** they still belong to an enabled focused button, link or `role="button"`. — *Why:* after an answer by click, WebKit keeps focus on the answered option, which is now disabled, while Chrome moves it to the body. With Part A's rule, Enter and Space did nothing after an answer in Safari. — active
- **D-A206 · 2026-11-22 · `useSwipe` reports every press** (`onPress`, `onPressEnd`) and listens for `pointerup`/`pointercancel` on the window in the capture phase; `CardStack` loses `onDragStart`. — *Why:* the hold needs the start and the end of each press. A press released outside the stack, or on the next card after its own card left, must still end, or the countdown would stay frozen; listening in the capture phase means no handler inside a card can stop the event first. — active
- **D-A207 · 2026-11-22 · Wording and colour.** The Next exit is announced as "Next card" / "Siguiente tarjeta" (it was "Next" / "Continuar"). The tutorial's first row says "When you finish a task, the card flies up on its own." / "Cuando terminas una tarea, la tarjeta sube sola.", and its last line says "Try it: swipe this card away or press a button." / "Pruébalo: desliza esta tarjeta o pulsa un botón." The countdown bar is red after a wrong answer (it was green for every result). — *Why:* no "Next"/"Continuar" text is left anywhere, while a screen-reader user still hears that the card changed. — active
- **D-A208 · 2026-11-22 · `autoAdvanceMs` details.** Characters are counted as a reader sees them (code points). A result with a `score` (a game end) always waits 2.5 s, whatever its feedback. A result without feedback waits 0.6 s and shows no banner. The countdown starts when the result is reported (the banner appears in the same render), not after the banner's 160 ms slide-in. — active

## 6. Open questions

- **Q-A201** A correct answer leaves after 0.6 s even when its banner has a note: phrases show the gloss, the non-manual marker and the grammar note (about 80–220 characters), so there's no time to read it unless the learner holds the card. Should a correct answer with a note get reading time too, for example with the wrong-answer formula? — *Default: no. 0.6 s as specified (SPEC §3.4); it is one constant in `feed/config.ts`.*
- **Q-A202** Keyboard and screen-reader users can't pause the countdown, because holding needs a pointer. Should the settings sheet (F1) offer a longer reading time, or turning auto-advance off (WCAG 2.2.1, Timing Adjustable)? — *Default: not in A2 (R-A201).*

## 7. Risks, known issues and stubs

- **R-A201** Keyboard-only and screen-reader users can't pause or extend the countdown. A wrong answer is announced in the live region and stays on screen for 2.5–5 s, and ↑, Enter and Space skip the wait, but WCAG 2.2.1 asks for a way to extend time limits (Q-A202; for F1 or F2).
- **R-A202** Not checked on real devices:
  - real Safari 27, which refused automation ("Allow remote automation" is off); WebKit was checked through Playwright's build instead;
  - a finger resting on a card on a real iPhone or Android phone: the long press must not start text selection, a callout or a context menu, and the browser must not cancel the pointer (headless emulation can't show this);
  - whether the sounds are audible (the sound code is unchanged).
- **R-A203** In the tightest layouts (about 600–700 px tall, a phrase with a long note, options wrapping to three lines), the viewer's credit and the lower edge of its controls are clipped while the banner shows (D-A204). Acceptable for at most 5 s; new formats should keep banner text short.
- **R-A204** Screens up to 600 px tall (an iPhone SE in Safari, small landscape windows) hide the hold hint, so nothing there teaches holding.
